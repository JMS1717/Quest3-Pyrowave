# Quest 3 short live observations — 2026-10-01

The PWU2 repair delivered visible SteamVR Home frames on Quest 3 over both wireless
TCP/UDP and native ALVR USB. Audio and the two-eye scene were present. This resolves
the earlier black-screen symptom for these captures; it does not validate gameplay,
all scenes, sustained high refresh or a quality/latency advantage over Virtual Desktop.

Windows server/client 24aa67e (`quest3.pyro.5`) was used for the wireless captures.
USB used server 7aa77b2 with the same .5 client. All captures requested 120 Hz and
1000 Mbps 4:2:0, no foveation, except the separate Auto failure. The headset was kept
awake using the owner's temporary proximity override. Captures lasted 30 seconds
(45 for Auto); scenes, clock and thermal state were not controlled across cells.

| Transport / rendering | GPU decode p50 ms | Decode→fence p50 ms | Estimated ALVR total p50 ms | Client FPS p50 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Wireless TCP, full panel, Compute | 10.37 | 15.48 | 86.58 | 59.99 |
| Wireless PWU2 UDP, full panel, Compute | 9.76 | 13.88 | 63.83 | 59.98 |
| Wireless PWU2 UDP, 75%, Compute | 7.16 | 9.06 | 52.14 | 59.99 |
| Wireless PWU2 UDP, 60%, Compute | 4.76 | 6.41 | 47.58 | 119.96 |
| Wireless PWU2 UDP, full panel, Fragment | 7.40 | 11.42 | 58.13 | 59.99 |
| Physical USB / ADB TCP, full panel, Compute | 10.33 | 15.10 | 87.58 | 59.99 |

At 60%, the 1st-percentile client FPS was 59.98 despite the ~120 median. It therefore
did **not** demonstrate sustained 120 FPS. Full-panel completion exceeded 120 Hz's
8.33 ms frame period in both decode paths. Fragment is not selected by default:
earlier reference readbacks found greater numerical differences than Compute.

TCP/UDP total differences are observations from unmatched captures, not a causal
transport speedup. ALVR estimates tracking-to-predicted-display pipeline latency;
no optical motion-to-photon measurement was made. The separate UDP network field
is a residual that can approach zero and is not direct one-way latency. Early builds
did not count all pending-frame overwrites; their zero superseded counters do not
establish zero lost or skipped presentation frames. Later builds instrument this.

The USB peer was loopback, with native ADB control/stream forwarding and a physical
USB device, and short recorded video payload exceeded 1 Gbps. The wireless server
used the PC's physical 2.5 GbE address. Neither test establishes 2 Gbps sustainable
payload capacity. The ~1% payload excess traced to truncating a floating-point frame
rate just below 120 to 119; matching .6 artifacts round it and check whole serialized
frames. Short-window measured throughput can still exceed a configured rate because
the measurement interval and frame delivery timing fluctuate.

A separate USB Auto test (1000 Mbps ceiling, 8 ms decoder limit) collapsed toward
zero bitrate without reaching full-panel 120 FPS. Manual 1000 Mbps was restored.
The .6 build ignores the hardware decoder latency limiter for PyroWave; fixed
wavelet GPU cost cannot be cured by indefinitely reducing encoded bytes. Network
and encoder adaptation remain active.

Matching client/server d901f15 (`quest3.pyro.6`) was installed without replacing
Virtual Desktop's registration. A 30-second full-panel USB/Compute/CDF 9/7 capture
at manual 1000 Mbps measured 10.16 ms GPU decode, 15.11 ms completion, 79.53 ms ALVR
estimated total, 59.99 median delivered FPS and 1001.79 Mbps median video payload.
It did **not** sustain 120 FPS. A subsequent 45-second Auto capture with the same
1000 Mbps ceiling and retained 8 ms decoder setting measured 1002.38 Mbps median
payload and 59.99 median delivered FPS. It did not reproduce the previous ongoing
collapse toward zero, but had transient low-throughput frames and a 654.53 ms maximum
estimated total latency. This verifies the guard in this short USB test, not robust
Auto behavior across networks or sustained high refresh. Manual 1000 Mbps was restored.

Two additional .5 USB captures explored CDF 5/3 at full-panel resolution. CDF 5/3
measured 9.02 ms GPU decode / 14.19 ms completion, followed by a CDF 9/7 repeat at
10.28 / 15.08 ms. Both delivered ~60 median FPS. Clock/history were not matched:
the first run changed from 492 to 640 MHz, while the repeat started at 640 MHz.
These observations justify further comparison; they establish neither a controlled
speedup nor equal image quality, and neither path met the 8.33 ms completion budget.

## Matching .8 USB bitrate and decode-path trials

Client/server b0f2f5e (`quest3.pyro.8`) displayed the corrected upright local
overlay in both eyes. The owner confirmed the both-thumbstick toggle on .7;
.8 retains that input logic. These short 30-second captures requested 120 Hz,
2080 x 2208 per eye, CDF 9/7, 4:2:0, no foveation and native USB/ADB TCP.
Reported bitrate is video payload telemetry, not measured physical USB line rate.

| Manual target Mbps | Payload p50 Mbps | GPU decode p50 ms | Completion p50 ms | ALVR total p50 ms | Client FPS p50 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1000 | 997.30 | 10.56 | 15.51 | 87.47 | 59.99 |
| 1500 | 1504.60 | 10.71 | 15.50 | 89.29 | 59.99 |
| 2000 | 2000.97 | 10.87 | 15.38 | 94.91 | 59.99 |
| 2500 (outside published presets) | 2511.59 | 10.88 | 15.02 | 107.50 | 59.99 |

At 3000 Mbps only 126 graph records arrived during the capture and estimated
total latency reached a 553.90 ms median. That was a stalled/unstable stream,
not a successful 3 Gbps test. A higher target did not resolve the frame-rate
bottleneck. The native decoder serially records/submits/waits for each complete
frame; its full-resolution completion exceeds 120 Hz's 8.33 ms frame period.
See [decode-pipeline investigation](../docs/DECODE-PIPELINE.md).

An OpenXR performance Boost hint at 2000 Mbps measured 10.98 ms GPU decode,
15.51 ms completion, 95.61 ms estimated total and 59.99 FPS. Fragment with the
same hint measured 7.86 / 12.68 / 79.88 ms and 59.99 FPS. GPU clock was mostly
640 MHz. The hint was restored and Compute remains the default because Fragment
reference-quality validation is incomplete. These sequential runs did not control
scene motion or thermal history. They neither prove a clock cap nor sustained
capacity. The .9 two-worker TCP path is an opt-in architectural experiment.

## Decoder isolation and performance-level trials

A .8 USB test at 1024 x 1056 per eye (temporary Scale 0.6, actual padded extent)
measured 4.03 ms GPU decode, 6.43 ms completion and 119.98 median client FPS.
The 1st percentile remained 59.99, so it did not sustain every 120 Hz slot. This
is a diagnostic cell, not the requested native-resolution preset. Native resolution
was restored. It rules out a universal 60 FPS cap over this USB transport.

Matching .9 b93b3f9 native-resolution / 2000 Mbps / CDF 9/7 / Compute captures:

| Decoder workers | Completion p50 ms | ALVR total p50 ms | Client FPS p50 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 15.20 | 80.51 | 59.99 |
| 2 (experimental) | 28.37 | 126.34 | 30.00 |

Two independent contexts regressed and were immediately restored to one.
Concurrent decoder contexts did not cure the measured GPU bottleneck.

An ADB CPU/GPU level-6 request on .8 retained 640 MHz GPU frequency, 10.94 ms
median GPU decode, 15.27 ms completion and 59.99 FPS. A separate .9 GPU level-7 /
CPU level-6 request reported **690 MHz**, 10.12 ms GPU decode, 14.51 ms completion
and 59.99 FPS. The .9 capture included startup/reconnection and fewer samples;
these are not controlled clock-only speedups or sustained thermal results.
Numeric property requests alone do not confirm effective clocks or mode availability
on another OS. All original CPU/GPU properties were restored afterward.

An app-specific .8 detailed GPU trace separated ~8.7 ms compute Dispatch from
~1.2 ms Preempt and ~2 ms full-frame RGBA Render work. Additional eye-copy and
presentation surfaces execute afterward. Detailed profiling incurs overhead and
was disabled before subsequent benchmark cells. This supports optimizing decoder
and copy work rather than increasing network target indefinitely. `.10` exposes
the pinned research codec's Haar transform as a matched experimental option;
the following matching .10 measurements still did not sustain 120 FPS.

Private captures, room screenshots, device identifiers and session files are excluded
from this repository. The short observations are useful for choosing the next tests,
not a release acceptance gate. Latest-frame polling and the Auto guard now have
short matching-build live checks. Next: validate the client-local overlay, then
replicated cool/steady-state tests and matched source/decoded quality measurements.


## Matching .10 native Haar comparison

Client/server c9285ba, 30 seconds per cell, native 2080 x 2208 per eye,
120 Hz accepted, 4:2:0, Compute, one worker, manual 2000 Mbps, same USB/TCP route:

| Transform / GPU request | GPU decode p50 ms | Completion p50 ms | Estimated total p50 ms | Payload p50 Mbps | Client FPS p50 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| CDF 9/7 / original properties | 11.24 | 15.20 | 85.49 | 2002.85 | 59.99 |
| Haar / original properties | 8.90 | 13.70 | 81.24 | 2006.12 | 59.99 |
| Haar / GPU 7, CPU 6 | 8.33 | 12.91 | 79.67 | 2002.24 | 60.00 |

The final cell reported 690 MHz. Each cell's host present cadence was ~120 FPS.
Haar reduced measured decode cost in these sequential short captures, but
completion still exceeded 8.33 ms and every cell's 1st percentile client FPS
was ~60 or lower. No sustained native 120 FPS or thermal endurance was established.
The screenshots showed SteamVR Home and the upright overlay in both eyes;
this is a visual check, not a reference-quality or optical latency measurement.
Original performance properties were restored after the clock experiment.

`.11` tests a pair-local Haar inverse: no unused apron reads, shared-memory tile,
or workgroup barriers. It retains the existing level dispatches and image barriers,
FP16 storage rounding, complete-frame fence and buffer lease protection. It does
not alter the CDF 9/7 default. Frame rate and reference-image acceptance require
matching cloud-built binaries and device measurements.


The matching `.11` source [3c223eb](https://github.com/JMS1717/Quest3-Pyrowave/commit/3c223ebee0075e56b65761db8fbd5bb48627778b)
passed [Android, Windows and regression jobs](https://github.com/JMS1717/Quest3-Pyrowave/actions/runs/36926939241).
APK certificate and both artifact manifests were verified. Two on-device synthetic
readbacks against the previous codec stayed within one YUV code value with no
source PSNR loss; details and reproduction are in the decoder investigation.
The new APK/server pair has not been deployed or live-FPS accepted. Quest returned
to its system tracking prompt before that trial; proximity and performance
property overrides were restored while waiting for the owner to clear it.
